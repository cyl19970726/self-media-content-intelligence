## Ground-truth shape

The video argues that virality across niches comes primarily from a core expression that activates shared emotional memory, rather than from hooks, “internet feel,” memes, or titles. It uses three parallel examples:

1. Youth confusion: “奥德赛时期,” linked to Qu Yuan and Wang Bo, is generalized into a shared human emotional memory.
2. Gendered injustice: the “莉拉坐” graduation pose is linked to Lila’s denied education and expanded into a collective memory of unfair treatment of women in education and work.
3. National memory: a highly liked drawing/post is used to claim that historical memory and pride can cross age, gender, and identity.

The ending reframes self-media as art: short video is only a form, the platform only a medium, and core expression is said to work across poetry, books, and film. It then strengthens the opening claim by saying virality and data follow naturally when work resonates with users.

The three cases are parallel argumentative illustrations, not a procedure. The edit repeatedly returns to the same masked presenter and setting, with three short Xiaohongshu interface inserts. Ten technical shots must not be converted into ten semantic scenes.
