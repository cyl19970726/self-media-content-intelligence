# Directing closures

Use this contract for account, single-post and series work. A polished script without these decisions is incomplete.

## Account closure

1. `identityPromise`: who we serve, what we solve, why us, what repeats.
2. `identitySystem`: comprehensible identity word, attitude, repeatable visual/verbal symbol, scoped proof.
3. `assetAudit`: expertise, access, lived experience, proof, production capacity, distribution and constraints.
4. `contentPortfolio`: acquisition, follow conversion, trust/proof, search/save, commercial carrier, expression/taste.
5. `seriesContracts`: each series' audience, repeatable transformation, future value, cadence and end/renew rule.
6. `commercialBoundary`: free value, paid value, natural use cases and prohibited claims.

Do not force every account to fill every portfolio role equally. State deliberate omissions.

Test identity legibility beyond credentials: can a viewer freely recall relevant tastes, skills, habits, aesthetics or ways of working? Treat a real, bounded vulnerability or mistake as a candidate relationship mechanism only when it does not harm others, evade responsibility or fabricate weakness. Guard trust and negative response.

## Single-post closure

### Decision layer

- `primaryJob`: exactly one of acquisition, follow, trust, search/save, commercial, expression, or an explicit custom job.
- `guardrailJob`: zero or one secondary outcome that must not materially deteriorate.
- `audience`: person, stage, situation and current belief.
- `demandEvidence`: observed question/search/comment/behavior or a labeled hypothesis.
- `topicHypothesis`: if this person encounters this promise with this proof, they will take this intended action because…

### Contracts

- `clickContract`: why enter now; title/cover/first-frame claim must agree.
- `deliveryContract`: what is actually learned, felt, seen or decided.
- `followContract`: why this account remains useful after this post.
- `actionContract`: one primary CTA tied to the job; optional low-friction secondary CTA.

### Narrative logic

Use only the structure the topic needs. Common shapes:

- tutorial: outcome/problem → prerequisites → steps → proof → failure modes → action;
- argument: tension → precise claim → mechanism → evidence/counterexample → boundary → action;
- story: present tension → context → escalating decisions → irreversible turn → result → meaning/transfer;
- experiment: question → setup/controls → execution → observed result → limits → next test;
- comparison: decision criteria → comparable cases → tradeoffs → recommendation by condition.

Every beat needs a `cognitiveJob`: orient, create tension, define, explain, prove, contrast, relieve load, summarize, transfer or convert. Remove beats without a job.

For stories, record the starting relationship state, intended relationship state, decision that changes it and visible evidence of change. Keep temporal stages separate from emotional/value labels; “childhood / startup / crisis” is not the same dimension as “underdog / grateful / pioneering.”

### Proof chain

For each important claim record:

- claim and claim type;
- evidence object/source;
- on-screen representation;
- what it supports;
- what it cannot support;
- verification/authorization status;
- fallback if unavailable.

When speech points to “this image,” “this BGM,” or an embedded example, maintain a referent ledger: exact object, interval/panel, source, rights and whether it belongs to the current work or the nested example.

Prefer showing an observable state or operation over declaring a result. Results need scope, timeframe and denominator.

### Carrier and shot plan

For every beat assign:

- speech: relation, reasoning, feeling or precise instruction;
- image/video: demonstration, state, comparison, environment or human reaction;
- text: structure, keyword, parameter, warning or source;
- audio: attention, transition, mood or intentionally none.

Inspect non-speech audio as its own carrier. Record presence/range, source and rights, referent, possible function and tool limitation. Scope absence statements to the channels and intervals actually inspected.

For every shot: time/target duration, content, function, evidence responsibility, source/rights, transition and edit decision. Use `keep`, `compress`, `remove` with reason and target duration when reviewing existing material.

Stable framing is valid when the cognitive map moves. Add visual change at meaning changes, proof moments, overload risks or emotional turns—not at arbitrary intervals.

### Packaging

- title makes the decision/problem and distinct value legible;
- cover/first frame complements rather than contradicts the title;
- opening delivers immediate proof/tension, not generic greeting;
- body repays every opening promise;
- ending closes the promise, states boundary, and creates the intended next action.

## Series closure

A series needs more than numbering:

- durable audience problem and recognizable format;
- an episode matrix with distinct jobs;
- `futureValue`: progress, next object, new constraint, accumulating toolkit or unresolved question;
- entry episode and catch-up path;
- proof accumulation across episodes;
- renewal, pivot and stop criteria;
- directory/index and cross-linking plan.

Avoid making each episode repeat the same promise with a new example unless the example changes the decision.

## Production closure

Declare minimum viable setup, people, locations, props, sources, rights, capture list, edit complexity, deadline and fallback. Use an intent outline for low-risk solo speech; use detailed script/storyboard for teams, complicated shots, claims, safety, budget or rights.

## Enterprise and offer closure

For enterprise work, first select the primary goal: brand influence, direct conversion, recruitment/relationship, or a stated alternative. Do not infer it from company size alone.

For commercial content map:

`target buyer → demand evidence → product/deliverable → service/info/tool/hybrid type → content form → conversion action → delivery/support boundary`

Mixed offers must state which layer the content proves and what remains paid, customized or unsupported.
