# High, median, average, and low selection

Use tiers to compare mechanisms, not to declare winners and losers from nine posts.

## Candidate pools

If the user has not supplied tiers:

- high candidates: top visible-likes decile;
- median candidates: posts nearest the corpus median, preferably within the 40th–60th percentile;
- average candidates: posts nearest the arithmetic mean;
- low candidates: bottom visible-likes decile.

For small corpora, use equal thirds for high/median/low and calculate the nearest-mean anchor separately. Disclose the rule and denominator.

Median and average are intentionally separate. In a long-tailed account, a few breakout posts pull the arithmetic mean upward while the median remains the typical post. Report overlap between the two pools. Prefer distinct deep-set items when possible; if the same post is the nearest representative of both, keep the overlap and explain it rather than forcing a misleading substitute.

Calculate the nearest post's relative distance from the mean. If no post lies within 25% of the mean, label the anchor `mean_gap`: do not claim an average-performance cluster exists. Compare the nearest lower and upper neighbors to show the discontinuity. This is often evidence of a polarized or multi-modal portfolio.

## Comparison set

Choose 5–7 per group when available. Balance topic and format, duration, presenter/UI/result proof modes, account era, collection/share ratios, and obvious or puzzling outcomes.

## Deep set

Choose 3 per group by default: high, median, average, and low. When the mean anchor is `mean_gap`, replace the forced average trio with the nearest lower/upper boundary posts and at most one additional diagnostic sample. Each video must represent a distinct question or mechanism. Record group rule, quantitative position, why it is informative, represented form/mechanism, alternatives, confounds, media evidence paths, and reconstruction status.

Do not select only the top three or bottom three. The purpose is mechanism coverage.

## Interpretation boundary

Likes are accumulated public outcomes. They are affected by age, account stage, distribution, packaging, topic, and unknown promotion. Use them to define observed performance strata, not causal truth.
