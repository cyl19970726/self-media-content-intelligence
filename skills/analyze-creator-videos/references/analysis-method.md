# Cross-video analysis method

## Why analyze

Recover a reusable operating system: positioning, audience promise, portfolio roles across search/save/share/trust/commercialization, content and visual grammar, mechanisms associated with high/median/low public performance, and experiments for a new account. Do not imitate surface identity.

## Evidence ladder

1. `portfolio_fact`: reproducible from the normalized corpus.
2. `video_observation`: visible or audible in validated reconstruction evidence.
3. `author_claim`: stated by the creator but not independently verified.
4. `system_inference`: an explicit reasoning chain from cited evidence.
5. `unknown`: not established by available evidence.

External verification is a separate optional channel.

## Explain performance through mechanisms

Compare high, median, and low videos on user problem specificity, promise strength, novelty, proof-chain completeness, comprehension cost, information density, visual/narrative structure, audience breadth and fit, likely save/share/trust action, CTA clarity, and claim credibility.

Distinguish correlation, hypothesis, and experiment. Use “best-supported mechanism hypothesis” when public data cannot establish causality.

## Account positioning

State who the creator serves, what recurring job they help complete, what transformation is promised, what proof format makes it believable, what commercial role each lane may serve, and what credibility debt the system creates.

## Transfer to a new account

Label every tactic `copy`, `adapt`, `test`, or `avoid` depending on evidence, audience fit, rights, and causal risk.
